**Best for heavy seams on a budget: Singer Heavy Duty 4423.** A good fit if you piece small quilts and also sew canvas, denim and bags, and you want a sturdy machine, with a metal interior frame <Cite id="C0026" />, in the same price band as the CS7000X.

Singer lists 6.25 inches of sewing space between the needle and the machine's tower <Cite id="C0028" />, about half the Jazz II's 12 inches <Cite id="C0151" />, so this is a small-quilt machine. Its strength is feeding thick layers. It lists adjustable presser foot pressure <Cite id="C0029" />, a metal interior frame <Cite id="C0026" /> and a top speed of 1,100 stitches per minute <Cite id="C0027" />, a maximum that takes care to control. It lists 23 built-in stitches <Cite id="C0021" />, a one-step buttonhole <Cite id="C0023" />, an automatic needle threader <Cite id="C0024" />, a top drop-in bobbin <Cite id="C0025" /> and a free arm <Cite id="C0031" />.

Singer lists up to 25 years of limited coverage, with some restrictions <Cite id="C0030" />. It sits in the $200–$300 band, the same as the CS7000X.

The limits are the small workspace and the short stitch list. Quilting accessories are not part of what we verified for this model, so plan to buy a walking foot and a quarter-inch foot separately. Choose the CS7000X if you want those in the box.
