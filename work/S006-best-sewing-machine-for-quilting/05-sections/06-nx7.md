**Best large-throat computerized machine: Juki Kirei HZL-NX7.** A good fit if you want the biggest throat on a computerized machine in this guide and you are willing to pay premium-machine money for it.

Juki lists a 12 inch throat space <Cite id="C0181" />, the largest figure among the computerized machines in this guide. That is space for a large rolled quilt, and it comes with a wide table <Cite id="C0186" /> so the quilt has a larger flat surface to rest on. For layered work, JUKI Smart Feed <Cite id="C0184" /> is a dual feed that moves the top layer in step with the lower one, which reduces the shifting that causes puckers in a quilt sandwich.

It lists 351 built-in stitches <Cite id="C0179" /> and 20 buttonholes <Cite id="C0180" />, so it works for garments as well. Top speed is 1,050 stitches per minute <Cite id="C0182" />. Juki lists automatic thread trimming, needle up/down, a drop-in bobbin and a free arm <Cite id="C0185" />. At 33 lb <Cite id="C0183" />, it is portable but not light.

Juki America lists a limited warranty of 2 years on electronics and 5 years on other parts <Cite id="C0187" />, covering the original purchaser for household use in the US and Canada. Juki sells through authorized dealers, so expect to buy and get service through one. We could not verify a price band: Juki's page shows a suggested retail price and dealer listings were inconsistent, so ask a dealer.

The limits are cost and complexity. A computerized machine adds menus to learn, and for straight-line piecing alone a Juki TL model costs less.
