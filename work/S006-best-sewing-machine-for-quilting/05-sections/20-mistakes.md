## Common mistakes, and who should look elsewhere

**Buying on stitch count.** The Jazz II lists 28 stitches <Cite id="C0149" /> and still suits quilting well. If you only piece and quilt, put the money into workspace and feeding instead.

**Forgetting the workspace.** A machine can sew beautifully and still be miserable with a big quilt. If you plan bed quilts, favor a verified workspace of 11 to 12 inches, as listed for the BQ2500 <Cite id="C0125" /> and the Jazz II <Cite id="C0151" />, over small-quilt machines such as the Singer 4423 <Cite id="C0028" />.

**Skipping the walking foot.** Layers shift without one. Check that your machine includes a walking foot or dual feed, or budget for one.

**Expecting one machine to do everything.** Straight-stitch machines are fast and focused, but they cannot sew buttonholes <Cite id="C0160" />. Decide first whether you need that.

**Ignoring weight and where it lives.** The BQ2500 and BQ3100 list about 34.4 lb <Cite id="C0127" />, <Cite id="C0140" />. If you move your machine often, a lighter model such as the Juki HZL-F600 at 21.6 lb <Cite id="C0191" /> is easier to carry.

**Who should look elsewhere.** If you want a longarm or a sit-down frame for large quilts, none of these is the right tool, and our [quilting machines hub](/quilting-machines/) is the place to start. If you mostly want to embroider, see the [embroidery machine guide](/best-embroidery-machine/). If you are brand new to sewing and unsure whether quilting will stick, a simpler beginner machine may be enough, as in our [beginner sewing machine guide](/best-sewing-machine-for-beginners/).
