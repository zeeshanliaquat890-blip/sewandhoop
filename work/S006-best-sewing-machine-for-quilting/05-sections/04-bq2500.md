**Best overall: Brother Quilt Club BQ2500.** A good fit if you quilt most weeks, also sew garments and home décor, and want one machine that covers both without a steep jump in price into premium territory.

Brother lists an 11.25 inch workspace <Cite id="C0125" />, which is room for a rolled bed-size quilt to sit to the right of the needle while you work. Quilting layers can shift as they pass under the foot, so the BQ2500 includes a Compact MuVit Digital Dual Feed foot <Cite id="C0129" />, a walking-foot style attachment that moves the top layer in step with the bottom one. An Automatic Height Adjuster <Cite id="C0130" /> keeps presser-foot pressure steady as the fabric gets thicker at seams, and a pivot function <Cite id="C0131" /> lifts the foot with the needle down so you can turn corners without stopping to reposition.

The machine lists 729 built-in stitches <Cite id="C0123" /> and 14 one-step buttonhole styles <Cite id="C0124" />, so it handles garment sewing as well as piecing. Top speed is 1,050 stitches per minute <Cite id="C0126" />, a maximum rather than a pace you will use for quilting. It has an automatic needle threader <Cite id="C0128" />, an automatic thread cutter, a knee lifter and needle up/down <Cite id="C0135" />, plus a 7 inch touchscreen <Cite id="C0132" />. The machine weighs about 34.4 lb <Cite id="C0127" />, so plan for a permanent spot rather than carrying it to classes often.

Brother lists a 2/6/25 year premium limited warranty <Cite id="C0134" />, with different parts covered for different lengths of time. Brother's page shows no price for this machine, so ask a dealer for current pricing.

The limits are weight and the learning curve of a touchscreen machine. If you only piece and quilt in straight lines, a Juki TL model costs less and does that job with fewer menus.
