**Best entry Quilt Club: Brother Quilt Club BQ950.** A good fit if you want a machine sold as a quilting model, with quilting feet in the box, and you will mostly make small to mid-size quilts.

Brother lists a 4.1 x 8.3 inch workspace <Cite id="C0116" />, much smaller than the BQ2500's 11.25 inches <Cite id="C0125" />, so it suits wall hangings, baby quilts and table runners better than bed quilts. Quilting accessories are generous: 15 included feet, among them a walking foot and a 1/4 inch quilting foot with guide <Cite id="C0118" />. The Quilters Bundle adds a two-spool thread stand, a straight stitch foot and a straight stitch needle plate <Cite id="C0121" />. Brother lists a manual drop feed <Cite id="C0122" />.

It lists 240 built-in stitches <Cite id="C0114" /> and 10 one-step buttonholes <Cite id="C0115" />, so it also works for garments. Top speed is 850 stitches per minute <Cite id="C0117" />. It lists needle up/down, an automatic thread cutter and a knee lifter <Cite id="C0120" />. Brother lists a 2/6/25 year premium limited warranty <Cite id="C0119" />. Brother's page shows no price and we did not verify a band.

The limit is the smaller workspace. If you plan bed-size quilts, the larger BQ2500, Jazz II or NX7 give you more room, and a quilter who outgrows a small workspace may regret the saving.
