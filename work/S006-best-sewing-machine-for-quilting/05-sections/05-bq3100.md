**Best for guided straight lines: Brother Quilt Club BQ3100.** A good fit if you already know you like the BQ2500's size and want extra help keeping decorative stitch rows and seam lines straight.

Brother lists the same 11.25 inch workspace <Cite id="C0138" />, 729 built-in stitches <Cite id="C0136" /> and 14 one-step buttonholes <Cite id="C0137" /> as the BQ2500, so the extra money here goes to extras rather than to a bigger machine. The main extra is a Sew Straight Laser Vision Guide, along with a V-Sonic sensor pen <Cite id="C0142" />. The laser projects a line you can follow, which helps when you are aligning rows of decorative stitching or sewing an even distance from an edge. The pen lets you touch the fabric to set needle placement.

It also includes the Compact MuVit Digital Dual Feed foot <Cite id="C0143" /> and lists 17 included presser feet <Cite id="C0144" />, two more than the BQ2500. It has a 7 inch touchscreen and a USB port <Cite id="C0145" />, and an automatic needle threader <Cite id="C0141" />. Top speed is 1,050 stitches per minute <Cite id="C0139" /> and weight is about 34.4 lb <Cite id="C0140" />, the same as its sibling.

Brother lists a 2/6/25 year premium limited warranty <Cite id="C0146" />. Brother's page shows no price, and dealer listings we saw did not agree, so we do not give a band.

The limit is simple: if you are comfortable marking and following seam lines, the laser may not change your results much, and the BQ2500 gives you the same workspace for less. A sale price can narrow the gap, so compare current dealer prices before deciding.
