**Best budget pick: Brother CS7000X.** A good fit if you want to start quilting without spending quilting-machine money, and you are happy making smaller quilts and learning the walking foot.

Brother lists 70 built-in stitches <Cite id="C0001" /> and seven one-step, auto-size buttonhole styles <Cite id="C0002" />, so it also covers garments. For quilting, the box includes a walking foot, a quilting foot, a quilt guide and a wide table <Cite id="C0148" />. The walking foot feeds layers together; the wide table adds flat space for larger projects. Brother lists an automatic needle threader <Cite id="C0003" /> and a free arm <Cite id="C0004" />, and a top speed of 750 stitches per minute <Cite id="C0147" />, the lowest in this guide, which most beginner quilters will welcome.

Brother lists a 25-year limited warranty, with free phone and online support for the life of the product <Cite id="C0005" />. It sits in the $200–$300 band, the lowest here.

The limit is room. Brother's page gives no workspace figure for the CS7000X, so we cannot say how large a quilt it will take, and big quilts may be awkward to roll under the arm. Choose the BQ2500 or Jazz II if you want a verified large workspace.
