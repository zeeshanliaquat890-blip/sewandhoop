## Price and value: what each band gets you

Prices here are bands, in US dollars, and they move with dealer sales. Some makers publish no price at all, so a missing band means we could not verify one.

**$200–$300.** The Brother CS7000X and the Singer Heavy Duty 4423 sit here. You get a capable sewing machine with enough stitches for garments and basic quilting, and in the CS7000X's case a walking foot and wide table in the box <Cite id="C0148" />. What you give up is workspace: neither lists a large throat, and the Singer lists 6.25 inches <Cite id="C0028" />. This is a sensible band for small quilts and for finding out whether quilting is for you.

**$800–$1,000.** The Juki TL-2000Qi sits here, based on authorized dealer listings <Cite id="C0178" />. You pay for a straight-stitch machine built for fast, careful piecing. The value is high if straight stitch is all you need, and low if you also want buttonholes.

**$900–$1,100.** The Baby Lock Jazz II sits here, based on Baby Lock's own page. The value is a 12 inch throat <Cite id="C0151" /> and a knee lift on a simple machine, and you buy it from a local dealer who handles service.

**$1,000–$1,500.** The Juki TL-2010Q sits here <Cite id="C0170" />. The step up from the TL-2000Qi buys a thread-trim button and speed lever, which is worth it only if you will use them often.

**No verified price.** Brother's Quilt Club machines (BQ950, BQ2500, BQ3100) and Juki's HZL-NX7 and HZL-F600 show no price on the maker's page, and dealer listings we saw were inconsistent. Treat them as premium-band machines and ask a dealer for a current quote. A larger workspace, dual feed and a touchscreen cost more, and they only pay back if you will use them.

**What else costs money.** A walking foot for machines that lack one, an extension table, quilting needles, and thread. Machines sold through dealers may also come with classes or service plans that change the real cost.
