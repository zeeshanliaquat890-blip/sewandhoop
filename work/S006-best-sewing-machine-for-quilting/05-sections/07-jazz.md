**Best simple machine with a big throat: Baby Lock Jazz II.** A good fit if you want lots of room to the right of the needle without a screen, a long menu or a premium price.

Baby Lock lists 12 inches to the right of the needle <Cite id="C0151" />, matching the largest figure in this guide on a machine with a dial and just 28 built-in stitches <Cite id="C0149" />. There is one one-step buttonhole <Cite id="C0150" />. For quilting, that short stitch list is no handicap: piecing, topstitching and binding need a straight stitch, a zigzag and little else. Baby Lock lists up to 1,000 stitches per minute <Cite id="C0152" /> and a drop feed for free-motion stitching <Cite id="C0158" />, so you can lower the feed dogs and quilt freely.

The machine lists a built-in needle threader <Cite id="C0154" />, nine feet and a knee lift <Cite id="C0155" />, which lifts the presser foot with your knee and keeps both hands on the quilt. An extension table is optional <Cite id="C0156" />, so budget for it if you want a flat surface for larger projects. The listed weight is 27.5 lb <Cite id="C0153" />.

Baby Lock lists a 25-year limited warranty, with 10 years on parts, 2 on electrical, 1 on circuit boards and 1 on labor <Cite id="C0157" />. Baby Lock says its machines are sold only through authorized retailers, who handle warranty claims and yearly service <Cite id="C0159" />, so this is a machine to buy from a local dealer. Baby Lock's page puts it at roughly $900–$1,100.

The limit is that nine feet and 28 stitches mean little decorative range and no dual feed. A walking foot is an optional accessory <Cite id="C0196" />, so budget for one if you want to feed multiple layers evenly.
