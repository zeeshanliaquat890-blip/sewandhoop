**Best lower-cost straight stitch: Juki TL-2000Qi.** A good fit if the TL-2010Q's job suits you but its price does not, and you are happy to trim thread with your heel instead of a button.

The two machines share a lot. Juki lists straight stitch only <Cite id="C0171" />, a top speed of 1,500 stitches per minute <Cite id="C0172" /> and the same 25.4 lb weight <Cite id="C0173" />, and an auxiliary table, here 23 x 14 inches <Cite id="C0174" />, for a work area up to 23 inches. The listed differences are in trimming. The TL-2000Qi trims thread when you rock your heel back on the foot control <Cite id="C0175" />, where the TL-2010Q adds a trim button. It lists an automatic needle threader, a drop feed switch, needle up/down, a knee lift lever and LED lighting <Cite id="C0176" />.

Juki America lists a limited warranty of 2 years on electronics and 5 years on other parts <Cite id="C0177" />, the same terms as its sibling. Dealer listings put the TL-2000Qi at roughly $800–$1,000 <Cite id="C0178" />, lower than the TL-2010Q's band, though the gap narrows during dealer sales.

The limits are the straight-stitch-only design and a smaller spec list than the TL-2010Q. If a trim button matters, step up. If a heel-trim is fine, the saving is real. Either way, you will want a second machine for buttonholes and zigzag.
