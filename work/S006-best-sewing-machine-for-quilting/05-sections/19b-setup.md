## Setting up any machine for better quilting

A few low-cost choices make any of these machines sew a quilt more smoothly, and they matter as much as the model you pick.

**Needles and thread.** Quilting runs through several layers, so a sharp needle makes a difference. A pack of <AffiliateLink query="quilting machine needles">quilting needles</AffiliateLink> is a small spend, and many quilters swap to a new needle at the start of each quilt. Use a good-quality thread in the top and the bobbin, and test your tension on a scrap made of the same top, batting and backing as your quilt.

**The right foot for the job.** A straight-stitch foot and a straight-stitch needle plate help with fine piecing, which is why Brother adds both to the Quilters Bundle for the BQ950 <Cite id="C0121" />. A walking foot helps when you quilt through layers. A free-motion foot is for drawing patterns with the feed dogs lowered, as on the Baby Lock <Cite id="C0158" />. Check which feet your model includes, because feet are not interchangeable between brands.

**A flat surface.** Support the quilt so its weight does not pull on the needle. The wide table that comes with the Brother CS7000X <Cite id="C0148" /> and the Juki HZL-NX7 <Cite id="C0186" /> helps, and so does an extension table for the Baby Lock <Cite id="C0156" />. Even a clear space on your desk or a folding table beside the machine keeps the quilt from dragging.

**Cleaning and servicing.** Quilting makes a lot of lint. Brush it from around the feed dogs and the bobbin area regularly, and have the machine serviced by an authorized dealer on the schedule in your manual. Juki's warranty terms list periodic dealer servicing as a condition for coverage, so keep your receipts.

**Take your time.** The machine does not do the quilting for you. A steady, slow speed and a rested pair of hands give better results than any extra feature, and most of the machines here give you a speed control for exactly that reason.
