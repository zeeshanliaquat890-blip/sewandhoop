## The ten machines, and who each one is for

<ProductBox id="brother-se700">
**Best overall: Brother SE700.** It is the easiest answer for someone who wants one machine that hems curtains on Monday and monograms towels on Saturday.

Brother lists a 4 x 4 inch maximum embroidery area <Cite id="C0032" />, which is enough for monograms, pocket designs and baby clothes. Those are the projects that teach hooping and stabilizer choice without wasting much fabric. It has 135 built-in embroidery designs <Cite id="C0033" /> and 103 built-in sewing stitches <Cite id="C0034" />, so you can start stitching before you learn to load your own files. The automatic thread cutter <Cite id="C0036" /> trims tails between colors, and the 10 buttonhole styles <Cite id="C0035" /> cover everyday sewing. The warranty is 1/2/25-year limited <Cite id="C0038" />.

The trade-off is the small field. A 4 x 4 inch hoop <Cite id="C0032" /> will not hold a jacket-back design. If you already know you want bigger motifs, start higher up this list.

<BuySkip buy="You want to sew and embroider on one machine and are happy starting with small designs." skip="You only want to embroider (the PE545 costs less) or you want larger designs from the start." />
</ProductBox>

<ProductBox id="brother-pe545">
**Best budget pick: Brother PE545.** The lowest band here, for people who want to find out whether machine embroidery is for them before spending much.

It is embroidery only, with the same 4 x 4 inch maximum area <Cite id="C0054" /> as the SE700. Brother lists 135 built-in designs <Cite id="C0055" /> and 10 lettering fonts <Cite id="C0056" />, an advanced needle threading system <Cite id="C0058" /> and an automatic thread cutter <Cite id="C0059" />. You can send designs by USB or wirelessly from a computer <Cite id="C0217" />, and the warranty is 1/2/25-year limited <Cite id="C0061" />. The box includes one 4 x 4 inch hoop set, an embroidery foot, a needle set and pre-wound bobbins <Cite id="C0216" />.

Speed tops out at 400 stitches per minute <Cite id="C0057" />, the slowest on this page. A beginner is unlikely to mind, since you will be watching and rethreading anyway. It does not sew seams, so you need a separate sewing machine.

<BuySkip buy="You already own a sewing machine and want the lowest-cost way to try embroidery." skip="You want one machine that sews too; the SE700 or SE725 does both." />
</ProductBox>

<ProductBox id="brother-se725">
**Best lowest-priced combo: Brother SE725.** Think of this as the Walmart version of the SE700, which can make it the easiest Brother combo to find in a store.

Brother's page lists it as a Walmart exclusive <Cite id="C0309" /> with a 4 x 4 inch maximum area <Cite id="C0297" />, 135 built-in designs <Cite id="C0298" />, 10 fonts <Cite id="C0299" /> and 103 sewing stitches <Cite id="C0300" />. It has an advanced needle threading system <Cite id="C0304" />, an automatic thread cutter <Cite id="C0303" /> and a 3.2 x 1.8 inch screen <Cite id="C0306" />. The warranty is 1/2/25-year limited <Cite id="C0307" />, and it sits in the $500–$600 band <Cite id="C0308" />.

On paper, its headline numbers match the SE700. Variants are different machines, though, so compare what is in the box and which file-transfer options each page lists before you pick one. We could not pin down every difference.

<BuySkip buy="You want a Brother sewing-and-embroidery combo at the lowest Brother price and shop at Walmart." skip="You want the standard SE700 listing, or a larger hoop." />
</ProductBox>

<ProductBox id="singer-legacy-se300">
**Best wide hoop on a combo budget: Singer Legacy SE300.** It suits a beginner who wants to stitch borders and long names rather than only small badges.

Singer includes two hoops, a wide 10.25 x 6 inch one and a small 4 x 4 inch one <Cite id="C0107" />. The sewing side offers 250 built-in stitches <Cite id="C0105" /> and 13 one-step buttonhole styles <Cite id="C0108" />. Singer lists 200 embroidery designs, some built in and some on an included USB stick <Cite id="C0106" />, plus an automatic needle threader <Cite id="C0111" />.

Check the software before you buy. Singer says the included software works with Windows only <Cite id="C0112" />, so a Mac owner should plan another way to create or change designs. The warranty is up to 25 years limited, with some restrictions <Cite id="C0113" />.

<BuySkip buy="You want a roomy hoop and a full sewing machine at a mid-range price." skip="You use a Mac and want the maker's own design software, or you prefer Brother's 5 x 7 inch hoops." />
</ProductBox>

<ProductBox id="singer-se9180">
**Best large touch screen: Singer SE9180.** Beginners who learn best by seeing things on screen will like a big display, and this one has the largest of the combos here.

Singer lists a 7 inch color touch screen <Cite id="C0098" />, 150 embroidery designs <Cite id="C0094" /> and 10 fonts <Cite id="C0095" />, with 250 sewing stitches <Cite id="C0093" /> and a one-step buttonhole <Cite id="C0104" />. An automatic needle threader <Cite id="C0099" /> and a metal interior frame <Cite id="C0100" /> are listed, and there are 6.5 inches between needle and tower <Cite id="C0101" />, useful room for garments and quilts.

The included hoop handles designs up to 170 x 100 millimeters, about 6.7 x 3.9 inches <Cite id="C0096" />, so the stitching field is on the small side. The machine weighs 15.5 pounds <Cite id="C0102" />, and the warranty is up to 25 years limited, with some restrictions <Cite id="C0103" />.

<BuySkip buy="You want a big screen and generous sewing space alongside small-to-medium embroidery." skip="You want a 5 x 7 inch field or larger; the PE900 or SE2000 is the better match." />
</ProductBox>

<ProductBox id="brother-pe900">
**Best mid-size embroidery-only machine: Brother PE900.** A good first purchase for someone who already sews and knows they want mid-size designs.

Brother lists a 5 x 7 inch maximum embroidery area <Cite id="C0047" />, large enough for a tea-towel motif or a tote-bag design. It has 193 built-in designs <Cite id="C0048" />, 13 fonts <Cite id="C0049" /> and an automatic thread cutter <Cite id="C0052" />. Wireless LAN <Cite id="C0051" /> sends designs from a computer without cables, and embroidery speed reaches 650 stitches per minute <Cite id="C0050" />, a maximum. The warranty is 1/2/25-year limited <Cite id="C0053" />.

It costs more than twice the PE545 (the $1,100–$1,300 band <Cite id="C0214" />), and the extra money buys field size and design count, not an easier first week. If you are unsure you will keep embroidering, the cheaper 4 x 4 inch machines are a safer start.

<BuySkip buy="You own a sewing machine and want a dedicated 5 x 7 inch machine from the start." skip="You are testing the hobby on a tight budget, or you want sewing built in." />
</ProductBox>

<ProductBox id="brother-se2000">
**Best step-up combo: Brother SE2000.** For a sewist who wants a full-featured sewing machine and a mid-size embroidery field in one footprint.

The maximum embroidery area is 5 x 7 inches <Cite id="C0039" />, with 193 built-in designs <Cite id="C0040" />, 241 sewing stitches <Cite id="C0041" /> and 10 one-step auto-size buttonhole styles <Cite id="C0042" />. Needle-to-arm space is 7.4 inches <Cite id="C0045" />. Brother lists speeds up to 650 stitches per minute for embroidery <Cite id="C0043" /> and 850 for sewing <Cite id="C0044" />, both maximums, and a 1/2/25-year limited warranty <Cite id="C0046" />.

At the $1,400–$1,700 band <Cite id="C0213" /> it costs far more than the SE700. Choose it if you have already outgrown a small hoop in your plans, not as a first-time experiment.

<BuySkip buy="You want 5 x 7 inch designs and a roomy sewing machine in one." skip="You only embroider; the PE900 gives the same area for less." />
</ProductBox>

<ProductBox id="janome-mc400e">
**Best large field for a dedicated machine: Janome Memory Craft 400E.** It is the pick for a beginner who is serious from the start and wants room to grow, and who is willing to buy through a dealer.

US retailers list a 7.9 x 7.9 inch maximum embroidery area <Cite id="C0311" />, with 160 built-in designs <Cite id="C0312" /> and 6 monogramming fonts <Cite id="C0313" />. Embroidery speed ranges from 400 to 860 stitches per minute depending on the design <Cite id="C0314" />, and designs move in by USB <Cite id="C0315" />. That is a bigger field than either 5 x 7 inch Brother here <Cite id="C0047" />.

It is embroidery only and sits around $1,400–$1,500 <Cite id="C0317" />. We could not load Janome's own US page, so these figures rest on two US retailer listings that agree. One retailer shows a 25-year limited warranty <Cite id="C0316" />; ask an authorized Janome dealer for the exact terms.

<BuySkip buy="You want a big, square field in a dedicated machine and have a Janome dealer nearby for lessons and service." skip="You are on a small budget; the PE545 gets you started for far less." />
</ProductBox>

<ProductBox id="brother-nq1700e">
**Best to grow into at home: Brother NQ1700E.** This is for the beginner who is certain embroidery will be a regular hobby and wants a large field without moving to a commercial machine.

Brother lists a 6 x 10 inch maximum embroidery area <Cite id="C0078" />, 258 built-in designs <Cite id="C0079" /> and 13 fonts <Cite id="C0080" />. The LCD measures 4.2 x 2.4 inches <Cite id="C0081" />, and there is an automatic thread cutter <Cite id="C0082" /> and wireless design transfer <Cite id="C0083" />. The warranty is a 2/6/25-year premium limited <Cite id="C0084" />, with longer coverage on the first two tiers than Brother's entry models.

Dealer listings put it in roughly the $2,000–$3,000 band, a big commitment for a first machine. Most beginners will be better served by a smaller hoop first.

<BuySkip buy="You expect to embroider often and want jacket-back size designs at home." skip="You are still learning the basics; start with a 4 x 4 or 5 x 7 inch machine." />
</ProductBox>

<ProductBox id="brother-prs100">
**Best if you may start taking orders: Brother PRS100.** It is not an everyday beginner machine, and it is here for the new embroiderer who already plans to sell.

Brother lists an 8 x 8 inch maximum embroidery area <Cite id="C0071" />, 445 built-in designs <Cite id="C0072" />, 21 lettering fonts <Cite id="C0073" /> and speeds up to 1,000 stitches per minute <Cite id="C0074" />. The screen is a 7 inch diagonal LCD <Cite id="C0075" />, and the automatic thread cutter <Cite id="C0076" /> is included. The warranty is a 2/6/25-year premium limited <Cite id="C0077" />.

It is a single-needle machine, so you stop to change thread for each color <Cite id="C0220" />. It sits in the $3,500–$4,500 band <Cite id="C0215" />, the highest here.

<BuySkip buy="You plan to sell embroidered items soon and want speed and a large design library." skip="You are a hobbyist; any machine above it in this list costs far less." />
</ProductBox>
