## What to look for before you buy an embroidery machine

**Combo or embroidery-only.** A sewing-and-embroidery combo does both jobs but makes you swap the embroidery unit on and off. An embroidery-only machine stitches designs and nothing else, which suits you if you already have a sewing machine. The SE700 and SE2000 are Brother combos here; the PE545, PE900 and NQ1700E are embroidery-only.

**Hoop size is the real size limit.** The maximum embroidery area is the biggest design a machine can stitch in one hooping. Small areas suit monograms and patches; larger ones suit jacket backs and big quilt blocks. Pick the area for the biggest thing you expect to make, not the average.

**Designs and files.** Built-in designs are handy to start with, but most people soon add their own, so check how files reach the machine (USB or wireless) and which software the maker supports. Software costs and operating-system limits are a common surprise.

**Thread cutter and needle threader.** An automatic cutter and threader save time on every color change, which adds up on multi-color designs.

**Stabilizer and supplies.** The machine is only part of the cost. Plan for stabilizer, bobbins and embroidery thread; for example, <AffiliateLink query="embroidery machine stabilizer cutaway">cutaway stabilizer</AffiliateLink> is a staple for stretchy fabrics.

Bands below come from manufacturer pages and dealer listings checked in November 2026, so treat them as approximate.

## Warranty, service and support in the US

Every machine here carries a limited warranty, but the lengths differ by part. Brother's entry and mid-range models list 1/2/25-year limited coverage <Cite id="C0038" />, while its NQ1700E, PRS100, PR680W and XE1 list a premium 2/6/25-year version <Cite id="C0084" />, and Singer lists up to 25 years with restrictions <Cite id="C0103" />. Read each maker's terms: the long headline figure often covers only part of the machine, not the electronics.

Where you buy can affect service. An authorized local dealer can often help with set-up and repairs and can let you try a machine first, which is especially useful for the multi-needle models. Buying online is convenient, but you will usually deal with the maker's support line. Keep your receipt and register the machine.

## Common mistakes, and who should look elsewhere

**Buying for hoop size you will not use.** Most home projects fit a small or mid-size hoop. A larger area costs more, and big designs take longer.

**Assuming the sewing side matters equally.** If embroidery is your goal, weigh hoop size, thread cutter and file transfer more than sewing-stitch count.

**Skipping the file check.** Confirm that the software and design formats suit your computer before you buy.

**Expecting a home machine to run a business.** Single-needle machines stop for each color change. If you plan to sell regularly talk to a dealer about multi-needle options. Machines for hats need a cap frame, and we did not verify cap-frame support for any machine here, so check the exact model before you buy.
