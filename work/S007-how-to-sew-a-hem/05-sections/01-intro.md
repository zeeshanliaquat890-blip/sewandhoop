To sew a hem, fold the raw edge of the fabric up to the wrong side, press the fold flat, and stitch it down close to the fold's edge. A hem is a folded and sewn edge that stops fabric from unraveling, and it also sets the length of a garment <Cite id="C0197" />. Done on a sewing machine, a basic hem takes a few minutes once you have pressed it well.

This guide shows how to sew a hem with a sewing machine, how to pick the right hem for your fabric, and how to fix the usual problems. It is built from published sewing guidance, not from our own sewing, and each figure links to its source.

## What you need before you start

- Your fabric, already cut and any side seams sewn.
- A sewing machine with a straight stitch and a zigzag stitch.
- An iron and ironing board. Pressing does more for a neat hem than any stitch setting.
- Pins or clips, and a ruler or seam gauge for even folds.
- Thread that matches the fabric, and a needle suited to it. Needle makers publish charts: Schmetz lists Universal needles in sizes 70/10 to 90/14 for woven cotton <Cite id="C0208" />, and Jersey needles in 70/10 to 90/14 (or Stretch 75/11 and 90/14) for cotton knits <Cite id="C0209" />.

<Callout type="tip">
Before you stitch the real garment, sew a test hem on a scrap of the same fabric. Brother's manual recommends a trial sewing with fabric and thread that match the project <Cite id="C0207" />.
</Callout>
