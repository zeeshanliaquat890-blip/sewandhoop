## How to sew a double-fold hem, step by step

A double-fold hem turns the raw edge under once, then folds the hem up a second time, so no raw edge shows inside. It suits most woven fabrics such as cotton, linen and light-to-medium fabrics. One university extension guide describes this "narrow double" method for wovens, which we follow below <Cite id="C0199" />.

<Steps>
<Step title="Trim and mark the hem">
Try the garment on or measure the length you want, then mark the hemline. Trim the hem allowance (the spare fabric below the hemline) to 5/8 inch for a woven fabric <Cite id="C0199" />. Allowances vary by garment, so a skirt may use more; follow your pattern.
</Step>
<Step title="Turn the raw edge under and press">
Fold the raw edge under 1/8 inch toward the wrong side and press it <Cite id="C0199" />. Use the iron's heat setting that suits your fabric and press, rather than slide, so the fold keeps its shape.
</Step>
<Step title="Fold up on the hemline and press again">
Fold the hem up along the marked hemline, so the turned-under edge sits inside. Press, then pin or clip every few inches with the pins pointing across the fold.
</Step>
<Step title="Stitch near the folded edge">
Set the machine to a straight stitch and sew close to the first fold, on the wrong side or right side as your pattern prefers. The extension guide then adds a second, parallel row that catches the upper edge of the hem <Cite id="C0199" />. Use the edge of the presser foot as a guide and keep your eyes on the fold, not the needle.
</Step>
<Step title="Press the finished hem">
Give the hem a final press from the wrong side. This sets the stitches and flattens the fold.
</Step>
</Steps>

A narrow, thin hem like this works on light fabrics. For a heavier fabric or a deeper look, the same extension guide describes a topstitched hem with a 1 1/4 inch allowance, turned under 1/4 inch, with a second row of stitching 1/4 inch below the first <Cite id="C0201" />.
